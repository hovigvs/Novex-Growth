What an AI Event Order Automation Actually Looks Like

2026-09-11 · 5 min read

The 30-Minute Task That Happens Dozens of Times a Month

Ask any catering manager what eats their afternoon and "building event orders by hand" comes up almost every time. A customer calls or messages with an event in mind, and someone on your team has to turn that conversation into a real document — menu selections, per-head pricing, dietary flags, logistics — usually 20 to 30 minutes of typing and cross-checking per inquiry. Multiply that by every inquiry you get in a month, and it's one of the biggest hidden time costs in the business, even though it never shows up as its own line on a P&L.

What Changes With AI Event Order Automation

The AI doesn't replace the person who approves the order — it replaces the 30 minutes of manual building that happens before that approval. Here's the actual flow:

  • A customer describes their event in a normal conversation — phone, WhatsApp, or your website chat
  • The AI extracts the real details: guest count, date, menu preferences, dietary restrictions, budget signals
  • It drafts a full event order — menu, per-head pricing based on your real price list, dietary flags, logistics notes
  • A staff member reviews and approves it in under a minute instead of building it from scratch
  • Once approved, a deposit payment link goes out to the customer automatically

Why a Human Still Approves Every Order

This is the part worth being direct about: the AI never sends a quote straight to a customer. Pricing errors and date conflicts are a real risk in this business, so a person always has the final say before anything reaches the customer. The win isn't removing the human check — it's turning a 20 to 30 minute manual build into a 30-second review. That's the difference between an AI system you can actually trust with customer-facing numbers and one that quietly creates problems.

What This Actually Saves

For a catering business handling even a modest volume of inquiries, this typically recovers 15 to 20 hours a month that used to go into manual document building — time that goes back into sales calls, kitchen planning, or simply not working evenings to catch up. It also means inquiries get a proposal back faster, which — as anyone who's lost a booking to a slower competitor knows — directly affects how many of those inquiries turn into paid events.
