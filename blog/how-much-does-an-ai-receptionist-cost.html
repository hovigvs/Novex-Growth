How Much Does an AI Receptionist Actually Cost?

2026-09-11 · 5 min read

The Honest Answer: It's Two Numbers, Not One

Most AI receptionist pricing — ours included — comes in two parts: a one-time setup fee, and an ongoing monthly retainer. If a provider only quotes you one number, ask what happens after launch, because the two fees cover genuinely different things and both matter to your total cost.

What the Setup Fee Actually Pays For

The setup fee is the one-time build: integrating the AI into your website, connecting your phone line and WhatsApp, training it on your real menu, pricing, and service policies, and connecting it to your CRM or booking system. This is real work that happens once, regardless of how long you keep the system running afterward — which is why it isn't waived or refunded as part of a trial. Waiving it would mean doing the full build for free and betting entirely on you sticking around, which isn't sustainable for either side.

What the Monthly Retainer Actually Pays For

The retainer is what keeps the system accurate and running after launch — not a fee for doing nothing. It typically covers:

  • Updating the AI's knowledge whenever your menu, prices, hours, or promotions change
  • Monitoring live conversations to catch and correct mistakes before they reach a customer
  • The underlying AI and voice API costs, which scale with how many conversations you're actually having
  • New features rolling out over time at no extra charge

Without an active retainer, a system like this gradually drifts out of date — wrong prices, outdated hours, nobody watching for errors. That's the actual reason the fee exists.

What Actually Moves the Price

The biggest factors are call and inquiry volume, how many channels you want covered (phone only vs. phone + WhatsApp + web chat), whether you want live voice or just text, and which additional automations you stack on top — things like event order drafting, dietary collection, or a loyalty program. A single-location business with modest volume costs meaningfully less than a multi-location operation wanting every channel covered.

How to Actually Think About ROI

The right comparison isn't "what does this cost" in isolation — it's "what does one missed inquiry, one lost booking, or one no-show actually cost me," multiplied by how often that happens today. Most businesses find that recovering just a handful of after-hours inquiries a month covers the monthly retainer on its own; everything beyond that is upside.
